Climate Change: A Scientific Overview

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns.
Since the mid-20th century, human activities have been the main driver of climate change,
primarily due to the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and natural gas.

The greenhouse effect occurs when gases such as carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and
nitrous oxide (N2O) trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere. This leads to a rise in average
global temperatures, a phenomenon known as global warming.

Key observed impacts include:
- Rising sea levels due to melting ice sheets and thermal expansion of ocean water
- More frequent and intense extreme weather events (hurricanes, droughts, floods)
- Disruption of ecosystems and loss of biodiversity
- Ocean acidification caused by absorption of excess CO2

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) has established that limiting warming
to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels requires rapid, far-reaching, and unprecedented changes
in all aspects of society. Net-zero emissions must be reached by approximately 2050.

Carbon capture and storage (CCS) technologies aim to remove CO2 directly from the atmosphere
or at the point of emission. Nature-based solutions such as reforestation and wetland
restoration also play an important role in sequestering carbon.

Adaptation strategies — building sea walls, developing drought-resistant crops, and
redesigning urban infrastructure — are essential alongside mitigation efforts, as some
degree of climate change is now unavoidable due to past emissions.

International cooperation is coordinated through the United Nations Framework Convention on
Climate Change (UNFCCC) and the Paris Agreement, which commits nations to setting nationally
determined contributions (NDCs) to reduce emissions.
