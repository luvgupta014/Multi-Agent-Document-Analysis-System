Climate Change Overview

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. Since the 1800s, human activities have been the main driver of climate change, primarily due to burning fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas.

Greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide and methane trap heat in Earth's atmosphere, leading to global warming. The concentration of CO2 has increased by over 40% since pre-industrial times.

Major impacts include:
- Rising global temperatures
- Melting polar ice caps and glaciers
- Sea level rise threatening coastal communities
- More frequent extreme weather events
- Changes in precipitation patterns
- Ocean acidification

Scientific consensus shows that urgent action is needed to limit warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels to avoid catastrophic consequences.

Mitigation strategies include:
- Transitioning to renewable energy sources like solar and wind
- Improving energy efficiency in buildings and transportation
- Protecting and restoring forests
- Developing carbon capture technologies
- Changing agricultural practices to reduce emissions

Individual actions that help:
- Reducing energy consumption
- Using public transportation or electric vehicles
- Eating less meat and dairy
- Supporting renewable energy initiatives
- Reducing waste and recycling

International cooperation through agreements like the Paris Agreement is crucial for coordinated global action on climate change.
